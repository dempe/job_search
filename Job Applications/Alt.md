I don't have a collection, so for me it's the money-movement problem.

At Dekstech, I built Node.js middleware connecting a Shopify store to its payment provider. Since it was touching payments, it was imperative that my code was correct. I scoped an idempotency key to each payment session and reused it across retries, mapped declines separately from transient gateway failures, and covered the flow with 32 tests proving a declined or interrupted payment couldn't leave an order paid but unfulfilled.

That was one provider and ordinary order sizes. Alt has the same problems at a much harder level: several rails, five- and six-figure transfers, and a marketplace where sellers are waiting on their money. Designing for the third retry and the duplicate callback is the part of backend work I enjoy most, and I'd like to do it where the stakes are higher.
