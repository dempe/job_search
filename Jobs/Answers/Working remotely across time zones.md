## Close (1)

Source: [[Jobs/Job Applications/Close (1)]]

> Describe your experience working remotely and if you have any experience working across multiple time zones in an asynchronous environment.

```
I worked remotely for a time at my previous company.  We also had people spread out across the world who worked remotely permanently. I never had any problems with this. If anything, such a setup highlights the importance of three things: communication, figuring things out on your own, and documentation.

Communication is the obvious one, since you can't get up and talk to somebody in Brazil if you're in Japan. At my previous job, I noticed that much of the communication was happening in direct messages. I urged people to move more communication to public chat rooms. This allows for two things. One, others can chime in if they know something even if they are not the person being asked. Two, if other team members don't know the answer to whatever is being asked, they can learn from the conversation. Communication needs to be frequent and as public as possible in a remote, multi-timezone environment.

Figuring things out on your own is maybe less obvious, but if the people you need to talk to are not available, you can't ask for help. I've always tried to ask for help only after I've fully understood the problem and can demonstrate various attempted solutions to said problem and why those solutions did not work. Such an approach leads to 1) a more constructive dialogue and 2) more learning on your part. This is one way a remote, multi-timezone workplace can actually foster independence and growth.

Documentation is a form of communication, and it's essential in a remote workplace. I was the only data engineer at my previous company. This meant that no one else specialized in what I specialized in (the data flow process). Documentation was, therefore, paramount, and I made sure to document all aspects of our data ingestion pipeline from the what and the why to (most importantly) the orchestration. (humble brag) My manager literally shouted with joy when he saw the documentation I wrote.
```

```
I've now worked in two fully remote roles. The things that I like most about remote work are 1) not wasting time on commutes -- I can get right to work in the morning, and 2) there's way fewer distractions when working from my home office. I feel like I can get more done.
```
